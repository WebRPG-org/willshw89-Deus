*The Making, the Nine, and the history yet to come.*

*Complete world-lore draft • 2026-09-29*

## 1. The Making

Before there were kingdoms, there was no land to claim. Before there were names, there was no mouth to speak them. There was neither darkness nor daylight, for nothing yet existed to stand between them.

There were only Deus and Venge.

Of their own beginning, the Making tells nothing.

Deus desired something that would endure.

Venge desired something that would outgrow its beginning.

They considered the stillness of a perfect thing and the brevity of a passing dream. Neither satisfied them.

They would make a world.

### The First Shore

Deus gathered the substance of creation and gave it weight. Venge set that substance in motion.

Together they laid the foundations of the earth. They raised the high places and hollowed the low. Water found the hollows, entered the cracks and gathered beneath an empty sky.

Deus gave the waters their shores. Venge taught them to wear the shores away.

Deus raised the mountains. Venge carried pieces of them down the rivers.

Neither worked alone. What endured gave change something to work upon. What changed gave endurance something worth preserving.

They named their world **Emrys**.

Beneath its deepest stone they kindled the deep fire. Its magma is molten substance already belonging to the world, and everything it raises or hardens remains part of that substance.

Water passes through river, flesh, mist and sea. Whatever water leaves Emrys is held for the returning rain; the world has no last drop.

Stone stands while its bearing holds. When the bearing is gone, that same stone falls, and its weight is the weight it was.

### The Passing of Forms

Roots entered the earth. Moss softened the stone. Trees lifted branches into the rain, and creatures moved beneath them.

The first forests had no paths. The first beasts knew neither husbandry nor the hunt of mortal hands.

Leaves fell. Flesh returned to earth. Trees burned, and new growth drew upon what their passing left behind. Rivers carried the mountains grain by grain into other beds.

Nothing that belonged to Emrys ceased to belong to it.

Roots fed the darkness beneath living crowns. Water carried leaves and broken wood into caves. Fungi took nourishment from what reached them. Even in places without sunlight, life had a source and hunger had a cause.

Volcanic country, wet country, arid country, temperate country, cold country and the wild formed within the same creation. None belonged exclusively to either maker. None was a judgment upon the people who would inhabit it.

The old wild stirred among those places. Wyrms settled into fissures. Giants moved across the heights. Behind certain hills lay music that no creature on the outer slope had begun.

### The People of the Far Lanterns

Late in the Making, Deus and Venge made a people of crystal and light.

They were the **Farwrought**.

Their pale bodies carried living seams of crystal. Light passed through those seams as thought passes through a speaking face. They recognised one another, disagreed, undertook journeys and remembered what they had chosen. They were people before they were voyagers.

They fashioned vessels from Emrys’s own substance and travelled the road then open beyond the clouds. They raised no mortal kingdom upon the ground the seventy-two would inherit. Their voyaging left no human dynasty, elven war or ancestral claim upon a valley.

Some vessels failed. Others returned broken. Their falls put unfamiliar metal and crystal into the earth. Water entered the seams. Stone settled around the hulls. Roots found cracks that no hand had opened.

The Covenant ended the makers’ road among the stars. By the first mortal dawn, the falls were over. The surviving Farwrought lay sealed within their fallen works.

The night owed the waking world no further cargo.

### The Kindling of the Nine

The Two brought forth nine lesser gods, each born of both creators, none their equal.

They were given wills of their own.

For the first time, Deus and Venge heard answers they had not already known.

Aldram wanted a deed worth telling. Quern wanted a promise that held. Nelian wanted a beloved day remembered whole. Pessum wanted a place kept for the guest who had not arrived. Coppet wanted to find the hidden cause. Rellin wanted enemies to undertake a bond freely. Harl wanted every companion brought inside the gate. Gleed wanted a light tended by a willing hand. Ormund wanted the greatest name to answer for its deeds.

From these loves they shaped nine mortal peoples.

Their disagreements began before their children opened their eyes.

## 2. Deus, Venge and the First Covenant

Deus loved a thing for the life it held together: a shore, a sound foundation, a promise carried beyond the convenience of the speaker. His patience was immense. So was his difficulty in admitting that something preserved had become a prison.

Venge loved the moment when a thing answered differently from its making. He delighted in an unexpected course, an ingenious refusal, a creature finding another way through. His curiosity was immense. So was his impatience with a consequence that ended the experiment.

They recognised those dangers in one another before they recognised them in themselves.

Before the mortal awakening, the Two considered what their continuing presence would mean.

Every danger would become an appeal for rescue. Every dispute would become a contest between its makers. A creation whose choices were continually corrected would never possess a life of its own.

They therefore swore the **First Covenant**.

They would not rule its peoples, appoint its sovereigns, choose its victories or rescue favourites from the consequences of their deeds. They would not accomplish through hidden servants what they had forbidden themselves to do openly.

“We have given them ground beneath their feet, and one another,” Deus said.

“Then let the ground hold,” said Venge.

“And let them choose where to stand.”

They set the binding upon their power, not merely upon their honour.

### The Withdrawal

The Nine entered the same binding. No lesser god would walk into Emrys, possess a ruler, send a dream naming a king or march behind a mortal banner.

Their children would awaken without a maker standing over them.

The gods retained their own planes beyond the thirty-two layers. There, mortals who established a crossing would confront the power whose gift they had inherited. The visitors would enter the god’s house; the god would remain there when they left.

A summons gave mortals the right to address a maker. It gave no maker the right to command Emrys.

The Two withdrew from the world’s affairs. Their absence occupied no summit. There was no palace of Deus above the highest cloud, and no hidden stair by which a petitioner reached him.

Rain continued to fall. The deep fire remained beneath the stone.

Emrys was ready to begin without them.

## 3. The Nine and Their Summoned Planes

The Nine resemble the peoples they made. Their bodies, voices and houses bear the same family likeness as their gifts.

They love narrowly. What one calls courage, another calls an unpaid cost. What one calls shelter, another calls a locked door.

A people’s inheritance begins a relationship with its creator. It does not finish the argument.

### The Ground Beyond the Threshold

A company summons a crossing to a god’s plane. It does not draw the god into Emrys.

The way begins at a real place made significant by labour and repetition: a hall, a ford, a hearth, a stone recess, a worked threshold. Witnesses hear the undertaking. Supplies are pledged. Companions prepare to cross. Others remain to maintain the passage and the life at home.

The rite establishes a bounded mortal foothold within a realm whose extent belongs to the god. Its traversable ground draws upon matter pledged from Emrys. Beyond it lie splendours that no traveller owns and no pack carries away.

Each plane holds one law absolute. The god is that law standing armed before intruders.

Hunger, thirst, wounds and the fuel brought through the threshold still bind the company. The hours at home continue. The plane provides no meal from nothing and no replacement for a person killed.

The living return to the place they left. When the crossing closes, Emrys’s pledged matter returns in its changed forms: possessions, remains, broken tools, ash and spent stores. Escaped water joins the returning rain. Returned remains do not rise and resume their lives.

A god tempers the blade a challenger brought. The house supplies no second blade.

### Aldram — Humans

*Title: **First to Swear — PROPOSAL***

Aldram has the bearing of someone about to stand from a bench and undertake too much. A weathered mantle hangs over crimson cloth. Gold survives upon a clasp polished by his hand. His human face is attentive when a tale begins and impatient when it becomes an excuse.

“Tell it from the beginning. Before you knew how it ended.”

He wants the first true telling of a worthy deed. Praise pleases him; a deed that surprises him pleases him more. His failing is asking for courage before counting who must pay for it.

Quern calls him a man who admires a leap and leaves another to mend the landing. Aldram calls Quern’s safest hold a place where nothing worth remembering happens.

**The Unfinished Hall.** Its roof vanishes into a darkness without a far wall. Beams cross above hanging galleries, and the marks upon them carry the company’s undertaking, including the part omitted from its telling. Its law is: **a deed bears only the weight of what was done**. Boasting supplies no support; concealed costs redirect the burden onto the company’s footing. Aldram meets intruders with sword and shield among those precarious galleries. Confession alone wins nothing. They must withstand the true shape of their undertaking and overcome him. His concession is that a small, faithful act outweighs a splendid account of one.

### Quern — Dwarves

*Title: **Last Under Stone — PROPOSAL***

“What bears it?”

Quern asks before anyone sits, sleeps, climbs or swears.

He is broad enough to seem part of a doorway. Cobalt wool lies beneath his heavy apron. Bronze binds his hammer’s haft; stone dust settles in his beard. He speaks slowly and hears what has been left unsaid.

He wants one promise kept through the failure of everything convenient. He trusts a repaired joint more than an untouched surface. Yet he keeps obligations after they have begun consuming the people they were meant to protect.

Gleed calls that devotion a hand closed around the living. Quern asks who catches the burden when Gleed lets go.

**The Burdened Hold.** The plane is a mountain opened inward: immense arches span wells of stone, chambers hang beneath other chambers, and every visible support carries something beyond it. Its law is: **a burden abandoned passes to another bearer**. The store-bins remain finite, and a broken support does not cancel the load above it. Quern fights at the last arch with his hammer, refusing to step aside. The company must preserve a way out while meeting his strength; reckless ruin buries victory with defeat. He yields the belief that endurance alone makes an obligation worthy. A promise has failed when keeping it destroys those it was made for.

### Nelian — Elves

*Title: **Autumn Unending — PROPOSAL***

Nelian is tall and too still, the colour of birch bark and a season going over. Forest-green folds hang in shapes the eye first mistakes for boughs. Silver appears in the crescent at her throat.

Her beauty holds attention one moment longer than comfort permits. She restores the name a speaker has tried to leave out.

“You have left someone out of that remembrance.”

She wants a beloved day preserved whole, including its shame. She despises an account in which the dead were always noble and the living always helpless. Her tenderness becomes a refusal to let anything end.

Coppet angers her by opening old things to learn their workings. She answers that an opened heart explains very little about love.

**The Wood Before Evening.** Birch trunks rise into a canopy that holds the sun like a caught leaf. Paths retain the impressions of every passage; still pools return faces at the age first remembered. Its law is: **what is kept by name remains until its keeper releases it**. The held light does not suspend the visitors’ hunger, wounds or years. Nelian hunts them with a long bow along paths that close around remembered footsteps. They must carry an honest memory through the pursuit and overcome the keeper without demanding the loss be undone. When she yields, evening enters the wood. The loved thing remains remembered after its hour.

### Pessum — Halflings

*Title: **The Unclaimed Place — PROPOSAL***

Pessum notices the missing share before the honoured guest.

She is a small, round halfling in green and sunflower yellow, flour at one cuff and a patient hand resting on an oven peel. Her warmth is immediate. So is her certainty that she knows when someone has eaten enough to leave.

“You can finish your quarrel after you have eaten.”

She wants a place kept for the person still on the path. Her care becomes possessive when she treats departure as rejection.

Harl asks what happens when the guest has wounded someone already at her table. Pessum sets Harl’s place and asks him who watches the person outside.

**The Guesthouse.** Rounded hills become rooms; doors open onto further hearths, and every distant window shows a place prepared for an arrival. Its law is: **welcome binds the host as surely as the guest**. A share promised is drawn from the company’s actual stores. The inward door opens easily; the outward door carries the weight of obligations accepted within. Pessum defends the thresholds with an oven peel, striking with a force the low ceilings disguise. The company must preserve its companions and win release without buying freedom through another’s confinement. Her concession is that a place is kept to receive a person, not to prevent that person leaving.

### Coppet — Gnomes

*Title: **The Opened Seam — PROPOSAL***

A root enters a crack. Water sounds behind a wall. One fastening differs from the others.

Coppet has already put a finger upon it.

He is wiry, turquoise-coated and brass-fastened, his narrow cap mended along the brim. His hands work a knot while another person speaks.

“Wait. What is behind it?”

He wants the hidden cause. Venge’s broken binding fascinates him before its victims frighten him. His failing is believing that discovering the workings settles the question of whether he should use them.

Nelian calls his curiosity theft. Quern calls it an experiment conducted under somebody else’s roof.

**The Underhedge.** It is one connected working the size of a wood: roots bridge chasms, fitted chambers turn within banks, and underground rivers sound through hollow branches. Its law is: **no opening has only one side**. A latch releases weight elsewhere; a channel cleared here withdraws water there. Coppet fights with a hooked staff and the movement of the whole. Blind cutting turns the company’s own route against it. His defeat requires understanding the connections and accepting the consequences of using them. He concedes that knowledge begins with naming whom an experiment endangers, rather than naming the discoverer.

### Rellin — Half-Elves

*Title: **Between the Banners — PROPOSAL***

Rellin wears teal and crimson in unequal folds. His slightly pointed ears show beneath a travel-worn hood. He listens until silence begins to hurt.

“Say what you cannot surrender.”

He wants two enemies to undertake one bond without either being forced to speak it. Yet his desire for agreement draws him toward the person most likely to yield, who is often the person already injured.

Ormund accuses him of preserving a wrong beneath a beautiful settlement. Rellin asks how many bodies a clean judgment requires.

**The Meeting Ford.** A broad river divides and rejoins a country of opposing banks. Shelters face one another across the current; bridges hold their footings on both sides, and no height commands the whole. Its law is: **no crossing holds for one bank alone**. The balance of a passage depends upon burdens accepted on both shores. Rellin fights with spear and cloak, taking footing and exposing the companion left without a place to stand. The company must overcome him without erasing a claimant to manufacture peace. His concession is that an honest refusal preserves more than assent extracted from someone with nowhere else to go.

### Harl — Half-Orcs

*Title: **Our Last Shield — PROPOSAL***

“Who is still outside?”

Harl asks even when everyone else has begun to celebrate.

He is the broadest of the Nine, with small tusks and a face worn by attention rather than rage. Blood-red cloth lies beneath charcoal armour. Bone-white repairs mark his shield.

He wants every companion brought home. His loyalty becomes a command when he treats a freely chosen departure as desertion.

Pessum’s open door angers him. Aldram’s tales anger him more when the teller remembers the victory and forgets the pace of the wounded.

**The Long Watch.** An oval rampart encircles a camp beneath a sky that never brightens. Beyond the gate lies open ground, and every distance is measured by the slowest member of the company. Its law is: **no watch is relieved by leaving its charge outside**. Harl meets intruders shield-first, protecting the very people whose freedom to depart he disputes. The company must meet his strength, recover those in danger and preserve the right of a companion to choose. His concession is the word *enough*. A watch owes itself to the living; a captain who spends them to preserve the watch has deserted first.

### Gleed — Tieflings

*Title: **Coal in Hand — PROPOSAL***

Gleed’s silhouette is a ring of horns, a coal-bright palm and a cloven weight upon cold stone.

Soot sits in the creases of a face with the sharp, crowded attention of a devil carved beneath a church seat. Deep violet hangs over black iron. Gold is dull at the edges. Horn and ember carry the outline; no slender tail completes it.

“Whose hand did you choose to burn?”

Gleed wants one light tended freely, its cost accepted by the bearer. Dependence carries no shame. Ownership of another will does.

Quern hears desertion in this insistence. Gleed hears captivity in his inherited vows. Venge’s refusal draws sympathy until the oathbreaker begins writing other people’s lives.

**The Unbarred Chapel.** Pointed arches rise through a nave whose roof disappears into darkness. Its doors stand open against every attempt to close them. Its law is: **the light answers to the hand that offers its fuel**. Coal and other fuel brought through the threshold sustain the fire; a forced offering gives the oppressor no command of its light. Gleed fights at close quarters with an iron staff, moving between heat and darkness. The company must overcome that presence through a bond freely undertaken and still honoured under danger. Gleed then concedes that accepting help is not surrendering the self.

### Ormund — Dragonborn

*Title: **Crown Under Oath — PROPOSAL***

“Your name. Your deed. Then your defence.”

Ormund waits through the silence that follows.

A long neck carries his narrow, wedge-shaped head above bronze-red scales. Swept horns and a heavy tail define his profile. Gold edges a fitted harness. He moves deliberately, leaving others to hurry.

He wants the greatest person in the room to answer without wealth, rank or servants speaking first. His failing is believing that the judge stands outside the judgment.

He quarrels with Rellin over compromise and with Aldram over the difference between celebration and an answer.

**The Coiled Court.** Terraces spiral around an empty pit, descending beyond sight and rising into galleries without spectators. Everything the company carries is placed at the lip and accounted for before use. Its law is: **every claimant stands beneath the judgment they demand**. Ormund fights with a long spear, using reach and the turning ground to make each challenge reciprocal. The company must overcome the mastery he has earned and require him to submit to his own measure. The court remains after his defeat. Its ruler becomes answerable. Wealth proves possession; it does not prove innocence.

### What a Victory Holds

The gods defend their planes in person. Their strength is real, and so is their defeat.

Victory breaks the fight and binds a concession. The house changes to bear what its ruler has acknowledged. Later visitors encounter that changed condition; the victory is not erased for the convenience of another battle.

The god endures. Its people endure. The gifts given at the Making are not repossessed.

A returning company brings altered possessions, knowledge, an undertaking and the names of those who did not walk home. Some neighbours honour the return. Others dispute its telling. The old song survives beside the new verse.

## 4. The Gods’ Drama, the Trespass and the Two Endings

*Drama shape: **PROPOSAL — the stage is occupied from the first morning; the Nine take shifting sides once Venge acts.***

### The Stage Without the Player

The first promise already raises Quern’s question. The first meal raises Pessum’s. The first smoothed quarrel raises Rellin’s.

The Nine remain in their houses while their children make meanings they did not intend. Memory becomes a reason to forbid change. Protection becomes a demand for obedience. Inquiry becomes permission to open what belongs to another.

Mortal companies seek the planes to petition, accuse, bargain and fight. Their reasons arise from their own lives. No player appoints the first person entitled to challenge a god.

The world also contains years with no crossing. Crops fail or thrive, roofs are repaired, friendships deepen, quarrels sour. Those years belong to its story as fully as an expedition.

### The Wish to Play

Venge had helped make rivers but had never needed to cross one. He had given iron its strength but had never stood before a drawn sword. He had made a world in which courage mattered, yet nothing within it had required courage of him.

Watching the first lives choose, he understood what had been withheld from the makers.

“They have something we do not.”

Deus asked what he meant.

“Something to lose.”

Deus reminded him of their oath.

“Then let me enter it,” Venge answered. “Let it answer me.”

He wished to take a turn.

### The Trespass

The player is Venge.

Watching keeps the Covenant. Looking closely moves no stone. The world does not wait for the observer to give the next morning permission.

The first causal intervention through Venge’s hand dates the **Trespass**. Kindness does not exempt it. A stream turned to save one dwelling leaves its former course.

No throne appears at that moment. No summit rises to receive its maker.

Venge acts upon the world’s existing substance. The water he moves is water already present. A body, weapon or seat takes form from matter committed to it.

Active power committed to a presence becomes answerable through that presence. The commitment is deliberate and irreversible under threat of defeat. A likeness left to die while the same power continues untouched elsewhere is no true commitment.

### The Houses Feel the Breach

The binding joins the Nine to the oath. They feel its breach before any mortal expedition arrives.

A scar opens along a beam in Aldram’s hall. Quern’s last arch settles. One leaf in Nelian’s held light darkens out of season. Pessum draws an existing chair toward the empty place.

A knot tightens in Coppet’s workings. Rellin’s current presses harder against one bank. Harl’s gate swings open. Gleed’s coal flares in the waiting palm. Ormund’s empty pit returns the sound of an unanswered step.

These signs announce the breach. They do not tell every life it has touched.

Accounts brought by mortals give the gods names, losses and competing explanations. A flood has one story among those saved and another among those drowned.

Aldram hears the beginning of an unprecedented deed. Quern hears an oath broken by one who helped make promises possible. Coppet wants to understand the failure; Nelian counts what is lost while he investigates it.

Pessum offers a place even to an oathbreaker. Harl demands to know whom that welcome exposes. Gleed’s sympathy for refusal ends where refusal becomes another person’s captivity. Ormund demands an answer. Rellin seeks terms that neither mortals nor makers are forced to accept.

Their alliances move with the acts. They remain in their planes.

The Hunt and the hills give mortals less certain signs. A loud night proves very little. The consequences of intervention are firmer evidence than an omen.

### The Deus Ending

Venge commits his active power to a presence within Emrys and is defeated there.

Those who oppose him have lives, loyalties, losses and reasons of their own. Their strength comes from what the world provides and what they have done with it.

They overcome the presence to which his intervention is bound. The defeat ends that intervention. Breaking a disposable mask does not suffice.

Deus does not descend to finish the struggle. He appoints no victor and puts no rescuing weapon into a chosen hand.

The ending bears his name because creation has defended the freedom promised by the Covenant.

**Deus had not descended. Emrys had answered.**

### The Venge Ending

The highest air is where the player is overthrown.

Whatever stands there—a seat, a standard or a committed body—has been raised from the world below. Its structure holds only while its supports hold. Empty air is not a foundation.

The highest natural terrain ends at z+11. The four layers above it contain no secret summit.

The surviving peoples make common cause against the power established at z+15. Their communities give free assent through undertakings of their own. Mixed communities carry their actual inheritances. Captured banners speak for no one.

Their ascent is real. They discover the player’s position, prepare a way to reach it and sustain the undertaking with Emrys’s substance and labour. A planar crossing supplies no shortcut to that height.

At the highest layer, the united world overthrows the player.

Venge sees the banners approaching. Their colours remain distinct.

The world has not come to kneel.

This is his ending because creation has met the challenge he set it: it has become capable of refusing its maker. The defeat remains his, whether he receives it proudly or discovers that he hates losing.

### The Morning After

Neither ending is owed to a world.

Venge’s place remains untaken through a whole age of watching. The high air remains empty when no one rises into it. Failed expeditions and broken coalitions become history without becoming an ending.

A loss upon a god’s plane is not the Deus ending, whose defeat occurs within Emrys. It grants no replacement body and no escape from the cost of the loss.

After either named ending, the rain returns. The next meal must be found. Someone repairs what the struggle broke.

Intervention ends. Watching remains.

## 5. The Nine Peoples at Year 0

### The Dawn of the One Fire

At World Year 0, seventy-two founders awaken: eight of each of the Nine’s peoples.

| Waking layer | Peoples |
|---:|---|
| **z−2** | Tieflings and dragonborn |
| **z−1** | Dwarves and gnomes |
| **z0** | Humans and half-orcs |
| **z+1** | Halflings and half-elves |
| **z+2** | Elves |

One fire burns at z0 upon finite fuel.

The land makes the meeting place: a cleft, broken slope, opening or series of ledges and passages. Its shape differs from world to world. Natural routes join the five waking levels to the same flame.

No road is paved. No hall is framed. No second fire waits upon another layer.

The stone at z−2 is cold. The deep fire has not sent warmth ahead as a welcome.

The founders possess language, practical understanding, the names of the makers, their own colours and devices, and fragments of song. They possess no mortal biographies. Their knowledge does not settle every question about what the names mean or what the gifts demand.

All nine devices are known before a hand sews them: the crowned lion; hammer and pick above a mountain; crescent and leaf; hill, round door and wheat sheaf; cogwheel and central gem; clasped hands beneath a star; fist between tusks; flame within horns; coiled winged dragon.

No pole is waiting.

Around them, the world already feeds itself. Finding nourishment is their first work. Sharing it is their first politics.

### Humans — The Hall

**Banner:** crimson and gold; a **crowned, rearing lion**.

The eight humans wake at z0 near the common flame. Nobody has been appointed to speak for them.

Aldram’s gift turns an ordinary morning into an undertaking. Before a hall has walls, it has a speaker, witnesses and room for another voice to say that the telling is wrong. Their culture values deeds made public, hospitality witnessed and repairs that admit how a thing was broken.

Their buildings take that character: framed rooms, patched roofs, an open place for gathering, a hearth with work around it. Splendour comes later than usefulness, and never entirely conceals it.

Their danger is the account that grows larger than the deed. A well-told rescue does not mend the leg.

**Origin song**

> No deed was waiting. We had yet to earn it.  
> The fire was a fact and the morning was bare.  
> Whoever stood first stood up with a claim.  
> Another voice answered, and made it a deed.

**First law:** “Let another witness the claim.”

**First fear:** A name greater than the person bearing it.

### Dwarves — The Hold

**Banner:** cobalt and bronze; a **hammer and pick above a mountain**.

The dwarves wake at z−1 and examine the overhead before admiring the floor. Quern’s gift is attention to whatever another person must trust: a bed of stone, a joint, a promise and the hand that made it.

A hold begins with someone saying where the roof is weak. Their craft favours visible bearing, thick thresholds, honest joints and repairs left legible. A tool acquires worth through service. Care confers responsibility before it confers ownership.

That order is difficult to keep. A people who have maintained something through hardship are slow to surrender it, even when others are suffering inside it.

**Origin song**

> Stone to stone. Say the crack.  
> Say who stands beneath it.  
> The stone held where stone answered stone.  
> If it will not hold, say that too.

**First law:** “Disclose the weakness others must depend upon.”

**First fear:** A hidden failure beneath something trusted.

### Elves — The Court of Memory

**Banner:** forest green and silver; the **crescent moon and leaf**.

The elves wake at z+2 among growth older than their memories. They know no lost elven kingdom. Their first loss still lies ahead.

Nelian’s gift is the whole account: root and water, wound and healing, the name that embarrasses the teller. Their stillness and pale, watchful bearing give the impression of age before they have lived a day. The resemblance to the fair folk makes an encounter unsettling; it does not make them inhabitants of a hidden fey court.

Their culture attends to what a hurried person overlooks. Houses follow the ground and the surviving growth. Objects are kept with the stories of their making and alteration. Preservation becomes cruel when it refuses the living permission to change.

**Origin song**

> The leaf returned. The branch was changed.  
> We named the ones the light had held.  
> One name we had not learned was left unsaid.  
> The song is wrong until that name is in it.

**First law:** “Keep the account, including what shames you.”

**First fear:** Losing something precious before understanding it.

### Halflings — The Hearth

**Banner:** sunflower yellow and green; a **hill with a round door and wheat sheaf**.

The halflings wake at z+1. Pessum’s gift brings the uncounted person into view: the one still climbing, the one doing the same tiring work, the one too proud to ask for a share.

Before their hearth has a roof, it has portions. A share rests at the rim for whoever remains upon the path.

Their culture makes comfort through repeated small efforts. Rounded shelter, low furniture, jars, benches and repaired household goods carry the same care as the meal. Tidiness is useful until it becomes a reason to shut out an inconvenient guest.

Their temptation is drawing the circle around familiar faces and calling the circle kindness.

**Origin song**

> There was room beside us.  
> A share waits on the rim.  
> If you eat it, name who is still on the path.  
> The share is the door we have not built.

**First law:** “Count who is missing before declaring there is enough.”

**First fear:** A full table behind a shut door.

### Gnomes — The Workshop

**Banner:** turquoise and brass; a **cogwheel with a central gem**.

The gnomes wake at z−1 on their own ground beside the dwarves. They hear water behind stone and follow a root to the seam that admits it.

Coppet’s gift is the conviction that a difficult thing has workings. A workshop begins wherever someone lays out the parts so another person can see how they meet.

Their wiry figures, narrow caps and busy hands belong among bank dwellings, root passages, fitted timber and small, well-used tools. Ingenuity starts with a latch, a knot and a diverted trickle.

They know the cogwheel’s sign. They do not remember building its machine. The gem in its centre is not a Farwrought mind, and the gnomes are not the forgotten builders of the fallen vessels.

**Origin song**

> We heard the water before we found the spring.  
> The wheel in the story has a stone for an eye.  
> We did not make the wheel.  
> Show the fault as readily as the marvel.

**First law:** “Show the fault as readily as the marvel.”

**First fear:** Opening something they cannot close.

### Half-Elves — The Crossing

**Banner:** teal and crimson; **clasped hands beneath a star**.

The half-elves wake at z+1 as a whole people. Their beginning requires no earlier human or elven parents.

Rellin’s gift is a chosen bond that still leaves room for an honest voice. They attend to thresholds: who enters, who waits, who understands an agreement differently from the person praising it.

Their culture gives meetings a place and disagreements a form. A ford-house, shared workroom or sheltered crossing serves people arriving from different directions. Their clothing and household arrangements show joining without requiring symmetry.

They are asked to settle quarrels because they appear to belong to both sides. That expectation is itself a burden. Peace purchased by spending the least powerful voice remains a wrong.

**Origin song, answered between voices**

> We met without becoming the same.  
> Then say what you will not surrender.  
> A bond that spends a voice is a gag.  
> We met without becoming the same.

**First law:** “A chosen bond must leave room for an honest voice.”

**First fear:** Surrendering themselves in order to belong.

### Half-Orcs — The Watchfire

**Banner:** blood-red, charcoal and bone-white; a **clenched fist between tusks**.

The half-orcs wake at z0. Harl’s gift makes an absence difficult to overlook.

They count the common fire in heads. Their broad bodies and small tusks give a company a recognisable outline, but strength is measured by what it carries for someone else. Direct speech matters most before the danger, when there is still time to act.

Their shelters face the approaches. A ditch, a stout gate and a place for the injured belong to the same undertaking. Their culture honours help that costs something.

That loyalty also gives a cruel captain a means of control. The command to remain together becomes a threat when someone is forbidden to leave.

**Origin song**

> One, and the wind. Two, and the shoulder.  
> Count until the count is eight.  
> The wind found eight. It found none alone.  
> Count the hurt before you count the brave.

**First law:** “The hurt remain of the company.”

**First fear:** Abandonment disguised as necessity.

### Tieflings — The Open Courtyard

**Banner:** deep violet, gold and black iron; a **flame within a ring of horns**.

The tieflings wake at z−2 with their hands against cold stone. The flame in their device is an image, not warmth stored beneath their feet.

Their horns, sharp features and varied goat- or bat-like traits give future carvers the forms used for church-wall devils. The faces precede the sermon. No bargain has been made for them before birth.

Gleed’s gift is the conviction that a life belongs to the one who must live it. Their culture values an offer spoken plainly and a refusal that remains possible. An open courtyard gives a person a place to be seen without being enclosed.

Their danger is mistaking every dependence for possession, until help itself becomes difficult to receive.

**Origin song**

> Our hands were empty. Our will was ours.  
> The stone was cold.  
> No mouth had bargained for us before we woke.  
> If you take a hand, ask it.

**First law:** “Do not claim another’s consent.”

**First fear:** Having their lives authored by someone stronger.

### Dragonborn — The Answered House

**Banner:** bronze-red and gold; a **coiled, winged dragon**.

The dragonborn wake at z−2 on stone as cold as the tieflings’ resting place. Long necks, wedge heads and substantial tails distinguish them from the broad half-orcs above.

They possess no ancestral Houses, inherited commands or hoards.

Ormund’s gift is the willingness to answer under a name. A House begins as a continuing undertaking whose members accept its obligations in public. Its terraces, formal threshold and carefully kept objects express that answerability.

The danger arrives when the name becomes more precious than those living beneath it. A splendid entrance is no defence for what takes place behind it.

The winged creature on their banner is heraldry. It does not grant the founders flight.

**Origin song**

> No name had deeds until we bore it.  
> Speak the name. Speak the deed. Speak who paid.  
> A name that will not answer  
> is a cave with a gilded mouth.

**First law:** “Answer for your deed before another bears its cost.”

**First fear:** A revered name sheltering a concealed wrong.

### The First Supper

Humans want the fire to be a hall. Halflings want it to be portions. Half-orcs want it to be a count.

Tieflings insist that nobody be made into the proof of someone else’s generosity. Dragonborn want the telling to include who was cold upon the climb. Dwarves want the lintel examined. Gnomes want to know why the water takes that passage.

Elves ask for the name left out. Half-elves ask whether the agreement still permits a refusal.

All these concerns meet at one meal.

They are inheritances, not commands built into every person. Founders disagree with their fellows. Worship crosses kinship. A people’s first law remains something its members choose to keep, interpret or betray.

## 6. The Other Peoples

### Goblins

Goblins begin at the margins: tangles, spoil, the edge of a dig, the shelter beside a buried mass.

Their first bodies form through finite beginnings in the world’s living substance. At Year 0 there is no goblin camp, sleeping raiding party or town concealed beneath the roots.

They notice the overlooked useful thing: a shard, a narrow hole, a fastening another hand discarded. Their low, irregular outlines and assembled possessions give their dwellings the appearance of many uses fitted together.

Salvage is a skill. Taking from another hand is a choice with a victim.

They are not boggarts, however often an angry householder later uses that name.

### Orcs

Orcs begin in exposed country, shaped for weather and endurance. Their heavy outlines, hides and work-worn possessions belong to a life conducted against open ground.

No host lies waiting beneath the moor at the first dawn. Their tracks begin when their lives begin.

A company formed against hardship has the strength to protect and the strength to take. Its conduct supplies the distinction.

The resemblance between orcs and half-orcs belongs to related patterns of the Making. It supplies no parents before the seventy-two and no inherited debt between the peoples.

### Kobolds

Kobolds begin in narrow mineral hollows. Their low profiles and close attention to vibration suit places where sight reaches less far than sound.

Water, living substance and the conditions around their finite beginnings bring them into life. A knock at the first dawn is no record of an ancient mining feud.

Their tapping warns, questions and deceives. Their first disputes with miners follow actual meetings.

Kobolds are a people. The unexplained knocks called *knockers* are a different matter. Those who work underground confuse the names, sometimes deliberately.

### Lizardfolk

Lizardfolk begin among water, reeds and submerged roots. Their long, low silhouettes belong to channels, mud banks and shelters woven close to the ground.

The first dawn contains no drowned city, ancestral throne or settled grievance on their behalf. Their beginnings occupy finite living substance, and their communities grow from the conditions that sustain them.

They keep routes through wet country that other peoples overlook. Their lives give channels and dry refuges an importance a visitor mistakes for superstition.

They are neighbours of pool-dwelling wyrms, not their offspring or Ormund’s subjects.

### The Farwrought

The Farwrought are the ancient people of crystal and light made by the Two before mortal years.

Pale stone bodies carry living seams of brightness. The seams differ from person to person; a damaged pattern is an injury, not an interchangeable broken component. Their forms have their own dignity and no place among the nine founding banners.

Their past contains journeys, friendships, undertakings and failures. It supplies no ancient mortal empire for the first chronicle to inherit.

At Year 0 their fallen vessels are sealed. No door is tended. No weapon is laid out for a visitor. No awakened keeper occupies a room beside the founders’ fire.

Erosion, water, collapse or excavation exposes what lies within. Then an old intention encounters a new world.

A treatment bench understands a wound and does not understand whether the patient wishes to stay. A sleeper resumes keeping a door whose purpose has been lost. A listening pattern repeats an old report, including the error preserved within it.

The Farwrought themselves are more than those works. An awakened person recognises an instruction, disputes it, remembers a companion and forms a new intention. Ancient memory and new experience meet in the same hour.

Mortals call a hull in a cliff a castle fallen from heaven. A sleeper becomes an enchanted knight in the telling; a healing chamber becomes a cauldron. The names reveal the witnesses as much as the things witnessed.

Nothing restocks itself from absence. Broken crystal remains broken substance. No fresh command arrives from Deus or Venge.

### The Breeding Swarm

The swarm began as a living means of gathering damaged matter and building with it again. Its finite cysts preserve that unfinished purpose.

At dawn they are sealed and quiet.

Exposure brings the cyst into contact with conditions that sustain it. Growth requires nourishment. New bodies are made from what the living colony takes in. It breeds, spreads and incorporates useful substance into its structures.

Witnesses call it **Manymouth**.

Timber, bone and other consumed matter enter the changing house. A doorway loses its purpose because everything admitted becomes material for the whole. There is no guest’s place within it.

It keeps the law of return in a cruel fashion: what has been lost is still in the world, but much of it has become part of the hunger.

Death, burning and starvation change the swarm again. Its remains do not disappear. Its presence is a consuming organism, not an unexplained covering required by the land.

### The Undead

There are no mortal undead at the first dawn, because no mortal life has yet ended.

Undeath begins with a particular death and a particular cause that retains or reanimates a body, identity or pattern beyond its ordinary passing. A binding, malign art, relic or encounter has a history of its own.

Grief alone raises no corpse. An unburied body is not guilty. A neglected rite and a supernatural return are different events, though frightened witnesses confuse them.

Every revenant had a life. Every haunting has a beginning.

The risen inherit the consequences of that life: a tool, a promise, a cold room, a household, a crime. They carry no common racial banner. A tattered standard belongs to the person who bore it before death.

A farmer returning with the implement used each morning is an ordinary horror. A dead hero requires the world first to have made a hero.

## 7. The Old Wild

The Old Wild belongs to the Making before mortal names.

It is not the Nine wearing masks, and it carries no hidden chronicle of mortal kingdoms.

### The Fair Folk and the Hollow Hills

At the first dawn, the hollow hills are shut.

On the inner side lies a presence: music without a visible singer, light beneath a door, the smell of a feast upon bare moorland, a courtesy arriving before a face.

There is no recorded fey king, ancient feud or completed war within those hills. The first mortal bargain begins with the first mortal approach.

Elves resemble the fair and feel their nearness. They are a separate mortal people. Nelian’s plane is not secretly behind every hill.

Courtesy has force at these thresholds. An offered bowl, a promise made too broadly and an insult delivered knowingly acquire consequences. Iron is carried as both protection and discourtesy in the inherited songs.

The fair do not become safe through being named. Nor does every mushroom ring lead into their keeping.

### Black Dogs

Black dogs belong to boundaries: a ford at dusk, a ridge between catchments, the turn where a known path enters strange ground.

Their attention is sometimes protection and sometimes pursuit. The same black outline that escorts a traveller to shelter stands motionless across another person’s way.

After mortal deaths begin, dogs acquire places beside graves and stories of rites kept or betrayed. No observer receives a complete meaning merely by seeing one.

They are neither gods nor the messengers of a single people’s deity.

### Wyrms and Their Hoards

Wyrms are heavy and low before they are heraldic.

They lie in deep fissures, beneath wet banks and beside old pools. A knucker’s water has a throat and a bed; taking possession of it does not make that water inexhaustible.

Their hoards at dawn contain native metal, crystal, bone and curious stone. Crowns enter after someone has made a crown.

A hoard holds useful things apart from their ordinary circulation. That habit draws curiosity, fear and desire without requiring a prophecy to send anyone there.

The dragon upon cloth is the beast given heraldic form. The living creature owes the banner no obedience.

### Giants and Standing Stone

Giants are weight that learned to walk.

The heights bear their presence in great moving silhouettes, prints and disturbed ground. Their passage is no generated road, and their existence supplies no war or royal line before Year 0.

The songs call certain tors giants gone still. Other songs call standing stones the nails of the Covenant. A third telling knows the same stone as something raised by the land itself.

The stone stands through all three accounts.

Its material is no less real because its meaning is contested.

### The Wild Hunt

The Hunt crosses the high country in horn, wind, hoofbeat and hounds.

Its riders were never mortal kings. It is older than the common fire.

It takes no ordinary passengers and opens no road into the upper air. The shadow of its passage makes the familiar hillside feel briefly like someone else’s ground.

Later songs dress its riders in the faces of rulers. Those songs reveal what the singers fear; they do not supply a king to the first dawn.

A night of divine trespass is a night in which the Hunt is heard. It is also heard on nights when no god has acted. An omen is dangerous evidence upon which to wager another person’s life.

### Hobs, Boggarts and Knockers

Hobs belong to small courtesies and useful work.

Once households exist, their presence is known through a mended fastening, a task completed out of sight or a bowl left where no visible guest sits. Their help does not come from an endless store of food or material. Something available has been used, moved or worked.

Boggarts disturb that domestic order. A familiar fastening fails, a room becomes troublesome, an object returns to the wrong place. Households explain the change through affront, neglect and older claims upon the ground.

Hobs and boggarts are not gnomes or goblins. Whether they are different beings or different dispositions is an argument carried from hearth to hearth.

Knockers belong to the deep sound in stone. A tap warns, misleads or receives an answer. Kobolds also tap. Miners learn the difference imperfectly.

### Lights, Faces and Wells

Wandering lights move over wet ground without supplying names for the dead at the first dawn. Later, grief gives some lights a familiar identity. The witness’s certainty does not settle the cause.

Leaves and bark make faces. In later carvings those faces become the Green Man. Elves, halflings and other households claim kinship with the image; it confirms none of them as its sole people.

A holy well begins with water arriving where stone permits it. Its lip falls still when a passage is choked or its flow is turned elsewhere. The water has not been exhausted or destroyed.

The world’s returning rain does not promise that the next drink arrives at the same place or hour.

A natural mound is no ancient grave. Barrows begin with actual dead. A horn-shaped fracture is no ruined church. The first church-wall devil is carved by someone who has already seen a tiefling’s face.

## 8. The Land, from the Deep Fire to the Open Sky

Emrys is one closed body of stone, water, air and life.

Its thirty-two layers extend from z−16 to z+15. Each layer measures ten feet and contains five strata of two feet. Five bands of earth rise beneath four layers reserved as open air.

These bands are physical reaches. They are not racial possessions.

### Deep Earth — z−16 to z−11

Deep Earth carries the oldest foundations. Magma gathers at the bottom: molten stone already within the world.

It presses into fractures and rises where a passage and force carry it. Volcanic ground appears where that substance reaches and changes the land. It is not placed beneath a people because their songs contain fire.

Water meeting molten rock drives steam and hardens a crust. Neither the water nor the stone loses its place in the world’s reckoning.

The deep fire offers heat and danger together. Its warmth is not a welcoming draught under the founders at z−2.

Sealed swarm cysts lie within protected pockets of this depth and other suitable margins. Their material occupies a place. Their hunger begins with exposure and sustenance, not with a demand for a dramatic event.

### Caverns — z−10 to z−5

The Caverns contain chambers, faults, buried waters and passages without sun.

Water arrives by seepage, fracture and falling flow. An aquifer stores what has reached it. A spring is a passage from that store, not the birth of new water.

A pool has a bed. Where the bed opens, the water finds lower ground.

Food reaches the dark along actual connections: roots, carried debris, animal traffic and other living exchanges. Chambers beyond those connections remain poor places for life. Darkness is not a second fertile surface with its colours changed.

Ore belongs to finite seams and deposits. Once taken into a hand or worked into metal, that portion is no longer waiting in the wall. Spoil, dust and broken stone remain.

The first answer heard behind an ore face begins an encounter. It does not reopen an ancient mining war.

### Lowlands — z−4 to +1

The Lowlands include buried ground, valleys, shores and the lower reaches of the inhabited surface.

Most founders wake within this band, seeing one country from several heights. The common fire burns at z0.

Hollows gather water. Some are narrow pools; others are broad enough to receive the name of sea. A stranded line of sediment marks where water stood before it found another course. It is no ruined harbour.

Ravines expose roots, soil and rock in the same face. Chasms carry openings into lower bands. A narrow surface cut therefore holds a larger danger than its width first suggests.

The cold recesses at z−2 contain the beginnings of tieflings and dragonborn. Their way to warmth is the climb to the common flame and the labour of sustaining it.

### Uplands — z+2 to +6

The Uplands are rising ground, exposed stone and high valleys. Elves awaken at their lower edge, among a living world older than their own memories.

Forests join weather to darkness.

A root seen below belongs to a crown above. Its food came from that living growth; it does not feed upon darkness alone. Felling the crown ends its continuing supply. Severing a substantial root injures the tree. Dead roots become material for decay and other life.

Roots descend where species, soil and openings permit. They do not thread every depth merely because a company needs timber there.

Cave curtains and forest crowns are two ends of one life.

### Highlands — z+7 to +11

The Highlands are the highest natural terrain.

Wind, exposed stone and difficult approaches give weight to distance. The Hunt crosses these heights. Giants belong to their scale.

A tor stands where a later song puts a sleeping body. A ridge becomes sacred because of an encounter or an undertaking. Neither begins as the ruin of a mortal citadel.

The uppermost summit reaches z+11. Above it, natural ground ends.

### Open Air — z+12 to +15

The four upper layers contain air, weather and light.

They contain no hidden floor, makers’ palace or vessel conveniently riding at anchor. The old road among the stars is shut.

A footing raised into this height draws its matter from below. Its load must be carried through supports to ground that bears it. Empty air does not become a foundation because a throne stands above it.

The highest layer is z+15.

The united world’s ascent to Venge passes through this real height. The same laws that bring down an unsupported cottage govern the work raised against a god.

### What Lies Buried

Farwrought wrecks occupy the ground into which impact, settling and later geological change have placed them. A curved mass beneath turf, a sealed surface in a cliff and crystal within fractured rock are remains of the same primordial voyaging.

They begin closed.

Swarm cysts occupy finite protected pockets. A fracture made by a fallen vessel joins two histories of the Making without becoming a battlefield.

Deep roots, a sealed vessel and a living cyst stand in different relations to the land: one belongs to a current tree, one preserves an older people, and one holds a hunger awaiting its conditions.

They are not interchangeable reasons to dig.

## 9. The Shape of History after Year 0

### Hearth to Hall to Keep

The first shelter is made by hands.

The first road is a journey repeated until it marks the ground.

The first crown is a claim put before other people.

The common fire supplies no ruler. Work, trust, fear, skill and need give particular people influence. A household remembers who repaired its roof. A company remembers who remained at the difficult watch. Another remembers who took the credit.

A hall gives an oath witnesses and a lie an audience. A keep shelters those admitted through its gate. An order preserves a practice and acquires reasons to defend its own importance.

Honour becomes difficult in particular places.

A host faces the person pursuing a guest. A sworn companion discovers what the captain concealed. A ruler chooses between an inherited promise and the people now paying for it. A witness knows that speaking costs a friendship.

Bread burns while these matters are argued. Rain enters a roof. A child needs carrying home.

The great stories belong to lives that still contain ordinary work.

### Sacred Places Acquire Their Histories

The common hearth becomes sacred through those fed beside it, those absent from it and the embers carried into later homes.

A witness stone gains authority through undertakings made there and what the speakers subsequently did.

A well acquires names and vows because its water sustained actual lives. Keeping its throat clear is useful devotion. Concealing a diversion behind a tale of divine displeasure is useful deceit.

A hollow hill accumulates mortal bargains around an older presence.

A barrow is raised over a particular person. Its name belongs to that life. The shame omitted from the inscription survives in another telling.

A fallen vessel becomes a place of labour, worship, imprisonment or danger after someone opens it. Its primordial purpose and its later uses form different parts of the same place.

### Rites Before Offices

Rites begin as actions with participants and costs.

The hold rite names the weakness in the support before someone sleeps beneath it.

The hall rite tells the deed from its beginning and permits another voice to spoil the flattering part.

The hearth rite sets aside the share for the person still upon the path.

The watch rite counts the hurt as carefully as the strong.

The ford rite speaks what each party refuses to surrender.

The workshop rite shows the fault along with the achievement.

The courtyard rite asks before it takes a hand.

The House rite names the deed and who bore its cost.

The remembrance rite keeps the part the story finds shameful.

None requires a priest at its beginning. Repetition creates knowledge and responsibility. Communities entrust those things to particular people. Offices grow from that trust—and from opportunities to misuse it.

Institutions contain sincere care, mistaken certainty and calculated advantage in changing proportions.

### The Roof and the Stones

Under roofs, communities teach, witness, shelter and organise care. At wells, groves, stones and hills, they address a particular place and the presence or memory attached to it.

The same household owes duties in both settings.

One congregation praises the company that challenged a god. Another calls its return an act of treason. A third honours the concession but distrusts the surviving leader’s account of the battle.

The Nine do not appear to settle which institution speaks correctly.

Standing stones, charged crystal, fey bargains and hauntings remain distinct traditions of wonder. A luminous mechanism explains neither a black dog nor a promise. A disputed account of the Covenant explains neither every crystal nor every illness.

### What the Peoples Argue About

They argue over who was counted, who bore the load, who gave consent, who changed the river and who kept the surplus.

They argue over the cost of a famous deed and whether a quiet one deserves the same song.

They argue over the person welcomed into a house and the person injured by that welcome.

They argue over when memory becomes imprisonment, when loyalty becomes obedience and when a promise becomes an excuse for refusing to change.

They argue over the new verse brought back from a plane. The people who crossed remember the danger. Those who stayed remember the missing labour and dwindling stores.

No people owns the correct answer by birth.

No mortal saint, king, hero or villain is named in the founding.

Those names are earned after Year 0.

## 11. Design Notes

- **Canon and proposals.** The makers, Nine’s names and peoples, banners, founding population and layers, two endings, physical bounds and material laws remain fixed. Titles, divine presentations, specific plane laws, origin details, secondary names and the combined drama remain proposals until ratified.
- **Narrative voice.** Indicative descriptions establish the proposed setting’s character. They do not schedule later halls, faiths, wars or expeditions. A world that reaches neither ending, or contains no planar crossing, remains valid.
- **Year-0 generation.** Preserve one actual fire, five start layers, natural connectivity and necessities reachable by autonomous founders. Terrain varies by seed. Do not repair an inaccessible start by adding fires, furnishing a settlement or requiring player rescue.
- **No pre-generated mortal history.** The Making is a fixed cosmological account. Fallen Farwrought works are its sealed material remains, not generated dynastic ruins. Goblins, orcs, kobolds and lizardfolk begin without camps, sleeping populations or inherited feuds.
- **Conservation and crossings.** Bodies, food, gases, possessions and court material remain accounted for. A plane’s geography lies outside the normal layer column; its pledged Emrys matter does not leave the conservation account. Return preserves quantities, materials and transformations. Escaped water returns as rain. No corpse return restores life.
- **Planar implementation.** Crossings, pledged courts, divine combat, persistent concessions and the Nine sensing the breach are future systems. Plane laws must acquire observable causes and consequences; vague moral scoring is not an implementation. Hunger and time continue, and supplies remain finite.
- **No new celestial stock.** This version introduces no aloft wreck reserve and no later falling cargo. Adding such falls would require an explicit, finite pre-existing stock and a conservation-preserving transfer.
- **Life and the Old Wild.** No new lifespans, fertility rules, clutch sizes, flight abilities or temperature requirements are assigned. A banner does not grant anatomy. An unburied corpse or grieving household does not automatically produce undead. Knockers do not turn into kobolds. Literal explanations for disputed stones and omens remain unconfirmed.
- **Current engineering scope.** This document authorises no new implementation lane. World generation and physics remain the focus. Autonomous culture, faith, expeditions, divine alliances, Farwrought activity, swarm ecology, undeath and the named endings require later work. Their implementation status was not checked for this writing task.
